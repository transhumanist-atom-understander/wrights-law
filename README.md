I'm addressing my fellow devotees of the god of straight lines on graphs, and I'm telling you to stop jumping to the end of the trend line.
Just because solar and battery prices are decreasing doesn't mean we will inevitably use solar and battery for everything.
When you look at the actual slope of the decrease, you'll see the massive efforts it takes to decrease the price.
When you want to do futurism, you have to consider not only what will happen eventually holding everything else constant, but what will happen first.
When you do that you'll see in particular that nuclear is not out of the game.

## Solar Wright's law plot

I'm going to post a plot you may have seen before, of the massive reduction in the price of solar power.
But I'm going to emphasize the massive scale of the effort required.

Using [OWID data](https://ourworldindata.org/grapher/solar-pv-prices-vs-cumulative-capacity), plotting the price of solar power as a function of cumulative capacity:


Of course the most impressive thing about this plot is the massive reduction in price, across multiple orders of magnitude.
But notice there's more orders of magnitude on the x axis than the y axis.
I've made sure that orders of magnitude are the same size on both axes, so we can get a visual sense of the relationship.

The slope of the line fit is about -0.4.
This would be very simple to interpret if it was -1/3 or -1/2, but it's annoyingly in between.
If it was -1/3, that would mean that on the plot, you would move three boxes to the right to go down a box.
Meaning, three orders of magnitude of scale-up to achieve one order of magnitude price reduction.
So a 10x reduction in price would require producing 1000x as many solar panels as have ever been produced.

If the slope was -1/2, then a 10x reduction in price would require a 100x scale-up of production.
As you can see, the denominator counts zeros.

It's in between... with a slope of -0.4, a 10x reduction in price requires a scaleup of about 300x in cumulative capacity.

That's how hard people fought for every order of magnitude in price reduction.
That's what I want you to keep in mind, when comparing solar to other technologies: that it will be cheaper once those massive efforts have been made.
But the rest of the world does not stand still, and massive efforts will be made on other technologies as well.
